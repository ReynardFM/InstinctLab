Instinct Lab · report

How it was built

A small world where creatures with tiny brains evolve. No one tells them what to do. The ones that do well have children, and over time they get better at staying alive. This report follows the project's major changes in order, from a single brain to a food chain that adapts to poison. Each plate says what we saw, what Rey decided, what was built, how it was tested, and whether it stayed. The worlds in the dishes run the real simulation in your browser.

By Rey Milson · work in progress, Nov 2026

Plate 01 · Oct 16–18 · changes 1–4

A brain that evolves

What we saw

The starting question: can a tiny brain learn to find food if no one writes the rules? Early on, a second problem showed up: the scores were mostly luck. A creature's score on one food map agreed only 0.28 with its score on another (1 would be a perfect match). In 500 generations, the best creature never stayed on top 5 generations in a row.

Rey's call

Build a world to watch evolution happen. Score every creature on three food maps, not one. And make every run exactly repeatable: the same start must give the same world on any computer.

What was built

Each creature has a tiny neural network. It reads four numbers: where the nearest food is (left or right, ahead or behind, how far) and how much energy it has left. Six middle neurons mix them. Two outputs decide how much to turn and how fast to move. The whole brain is 44 numbers, the strength of each connection.

Evolution: the creatures that ate the most become parents. Their children get the same 44 numbers with small random changes. The project also got its own math library, so every browser computes exactly the same bits.

Like a car with a steering wheel and a pedal, driven by a dice roll. Evolution keeps the drivers whose dice happened to point at food, and lets their children roll again.

Blue = positive, red = negative. Thicker lines are stronger connections. A filled circle is a neuron firing.

The test

Evolved brains against the obvious rule written by hand: "turn to the nearest food, full speed".

Who drivesFood eaten per map
The rule written by hand24.3
Evolved brains, after 100 generations42 to 47

7 worlds each, Oct 18–19, in the default world of the time (it already had some poison food).

What happened

Evolution beat the hand-written rule by almost double. One likely reason, not proven: full speed burns energy, and evolved brains learn when to slow down. With three maps per generation, the best brains now stay on top long enough to pass on what they have.

Kept or dropped
Kept All four are still the base of every later plate.

Plate 02 · Oct 21–23 · changes 6–7

Hunters, and a world that runs itself

What we saw

The prey had nothing to run from. Then, once hunters arrived, a deeper mismatch: in "generations", a judge ends every round, scores everyone, and picks the parents. Nobody gets hungry and nobody has babies on their own. Rey had pictured something else: a world like the game WorldBox, that runs by itself, where you watch the numbers of each animal rise and fall.

Rey's call

First, hunters with brains, starting at 4, with a bigger gene pool (12 hunter brains instead of 4). Then, a world with no judge.

What was built

The living world: no generations. Every animal burns energy each moment. Eating refills it. A full animal has a baby with a slightly changed brain. An empty or old animal dies. Plants have roots: an eaten plant grows back in the same place. If one side dies out, it comes back from its best brains so far.

Speed
The test

Do both sides really evolve? Brains frozen at generation 11 (early) and 131 (late) were played against each other, in 14 worlds, 15 maps each.

Against early preyAgainst late prey
Early hunters catch41%–
Late hunters catch87%56%

Does the living world last? Plants that vanish when eaten: the world collapsed in every world tested (the prey eat everything; no single prey gains by holding back). Plants with roots: kept going. Hunters and prey both alive for a stretch of 20,000 ticks: , and 12 of 14 on 14 fresh worlds.

What happened

An arms race. Late hunters are much better against early prey, and late prey are much better against early hunters. The catch rate looked flat over time only because both sides improved together.

Like locks and lock-pickers: both get better every year, so the number of break-ins stays about the same.

Kept or dropped
Kept The living world became the main world. Generations stay as a measuring tool.
03
Plate 03 · Oct 19–31 · changes 5, 8, 10

Poison, and a real eye

What we saw

Poison plants were a flat energy cost: eat one, lose a bit, move on. Rey wanted a real food chain, where poison moves from plants to prey to hunters, and the young and old suffer most. Later, a second problem: the creatures' senses were written by hand ("where is the nearest poison").

Rey's call

Poison that builds up in the body. Then the eye: “raw sense so they evolve their own smart sense”

What was built

A poison level in each body. It makes everything cost more energy and fades slowly (half every 500 ticks). A hunter that catches a sick prey takes in all of its poison.

An all-around eye with four slices: ahead, right, behind, left. Each slice reports how much plant, poison plant, other prey and hunter it sees; closer things count more. Each kind of seeing is switched on by a gene that costs a little energy, so evolution decides which senses are worth having.

What the ringed prey's eye reads right now. Darker = more of it, closer.

Speed
The test

Poison against no poison (Oct 24): with poison, hunters died out 19 to 46 times per run; without it, 3 times. Hunters made immune to poison: balanced again. So the poison inside the hunters was the cause.

Which senses did evolution keep? 14 worlds × 130 generations. The control is a gene that switches on nothing.

SenseCreatures with it on, at the endBeat the control by a lot
Poison plants92%
Hunters84%
Other prey2%–
Control (does nothing)38%–

"A lot" = more than 20 points above the control. In the living world (200,000 ticks) the same genes were slower: poison 7 of 14 and still rising, hunters 3 of 14.

What happened

Poison tipped the balance to the prey. The top of the food chain got the whole dose, again and again.

Like DDT and eagles: the insects get a little, the fish more, the eagle the most.

Evolution kept the senses that tell something new and have a move to go with it: see poison, steer away; see a hunter, run. It dropped the sense with no move attached.

Kept or dropped
Kept Poison and the eye. Dropped Resistance trained by eating poison: it stayed near zero (0.00 to 0.06), so it was switched off.

Side box: learning during life (Oct 19)

Could brains also learn while they live, not only between generations? At first learning made scores worse: the connections kept growing. Rey asked for two fixes (forgetting, and learning less when hungry). Forgetting fixed the harm, but learning still gave no gain. Kept, off

Side box: a game (Oct 26–30)

The project tried a play page with cards and terrain. Rey's verdict: “the logic is there but not the fun”. It was dropped on Oct 30 and the work went back to the lab. Dropped

Plate 04 · Nov 3–11 · changes 11–12

Hunters learn to dodge, but it doesn't pay

What we saw

Hunters were dying of poison. They could only tell a sick prey by its speed (poison slows prey down), and that clue was weak: in some worlds healthy prey moved just as slowly by choice. There was also no clear way to say whether hunters had "adapted".

Rey's call

A fair bar: compare against a control, over 14 worlds. Hunters must catch fewer sick prey and end up more numerous, in at least 10 of 14. Then a clearer clue: glow first, then scent.

What was built

A glow: sick prey glow, and the hunters' eye can see it. The control is the same world with blind hunters. (Scent was built too; it did worse and was later deleted.)

Speed

"Pick" measures how choosy hunters are: the share of sick prey among their catches, divided by the share of sick prey alive. 1 means they catch sick prey exactly as often as chance. Below 1 means they avoid them. It needs a few thousand ticks to settle.

The test

Glow against blind, 14 worlds of 200,000 ticks each:

GlowBlindGlow better in
Pick (lower = avoids sick prey)0.520.64
Hunters alive27.827.5
Prey alive8186

Nov 8, 2026. In a longer test (6 worlds, 400,000 ticks, twice the food) the gap kept growing: 0.43 against 0.57 in the last stretch. The two dishes above are one of the 14, so their numbers can differ a lot from these averages.

What happened

Hunters with glow learned to dodge sick prey, in 11 of 14 worlds, every time it was tested. But they did not end up more numerous: the bar was never met (best: 9 of 14). A better hunter eats more of the healthy prey, so fewer prey are left, so hunters go hungry again.

Like a fisherman who learns to skip the bad fish. He catches more good ones, the lake empties faster, and next year he eats about the same as before.

Kept or dropped
Kept The glow, as the clue for every later test. The bar itself was replaced on plate 7.

Plate 05 · Nov 11 · change 13

Making it natural

What we saw

Rey: “creatures die too fast”. Measured: a hunter caught 2 prey in its whole life (the middle value) and died after about 400 ticks. A prey lived about 380 ticks and ate 2 meals. Life was short and jumpy, and hunters often died out.

Rey's call

Slow down how fast bodies use energy, and check the world against nature's food pyramid. Then: “hunter will default slow”

What was built

Slow hunters: they burn energy 4 times slower, and each catch gives 4 times less. They need the same number of catches over time, but a full body now lasts 4 times longer.

Speed

Both dishes: hunters see the glow, poison is on, same start. "Died out" counts how often the hunters all died.

The test

The old proofs rerun with slow hunters, same 14 worlds, 200,000 ticks each:

Fast huntersSlow hunters
Hunter die-outs per run5.71.6
Hunters per prey0.360.19
Prey alive81119
World works (no die-out at the end)
Hunter deaths with poison in them32%69%

Glow, full poison, senses handed out. With senses left to evolve the die-outs fell more: 14.5 to 3.1.

Against nature: about 10% of the energy in one level of a food chain reaches the next, and big meat-eaters need about 111 kg of prey per kg of their own body (Carbone & Gittleman, Science, 2002). The world went from 36 to 19 hunters per 100 prey: closer to nature, still far above it.

What happened

Hunter die-outs fell 3 to 7 times. The pyramid got a narrower top. Long-lived hunters collect more poison over a lifetime, so more of them die with poison inside.

Like a bear and a shrew. The shrew must eat every few hours or die; the bear eats big and can wait. Bears' numbers swing much less.

A correction

We first read the results as "slow hunters dodge better". Wrong: blind slow hunters' pick fell too (0.47 against 0.64), and blind hunters cannot see sickness. With fewer hunters per prey, a sick prey more often dies by itself before anyone catches it. Only glow against blind counts, and with slow hunters that gap got smaller (glow lower in 6 of 14).

Kept or dropped
Kept Slow hunters are the default since Nov 11. Dropped Slow prey: prey lives in 12,000 ticks fell from 1,282 to 59 and the hunters starved.
